Headline finding: karat is everywhere, the word “solid” is rare — and regulated

Every one of the 59 listings states a legal karat or 750/1000 fineness across 10K, 14K, 18K and 24K. 15 of 59 explicitly state “solid gold” in the seller's own text, while 44 name a karat only. Under federal 16 CFR § 23.3(c)(1), hollow-center gold may NOT legally be called “Solid Gold” — yet Mejuri's FAQ and Bold Chain Ring call hollowed gold “solid gold” (flagged). See Flagged for review and the standalone 13-row evidence review at /GOLD1/site/ →.

What the checklist got right

  • Karat is stated on every listing. All 59 rows name 10K, 14K, 18K (or 750/1000) or 24K in the seller's official page.
  • All 10 return policies & BBB records are verified. Windows span 7 days (Menē, up to 10% restocking fee) and 14 days (Anna Sheffield, 10% restocking fee) to 30 days (Ross-Simons, Mejuri, Automic Gold, Cartier from delivery; Blue Nile & Brilliant Earth from shipment/readiness; With Clarity max 1 return/6 mos) — while Aurate holds a BBB “F” rating and displays “Final Sale! Exchange for size only” on all 5 ring PDPs.
  • Plated & dead links were rejected, not ranked. Set & Stones' gold-filled results and 4 dead 404 product URLs were excluded and logged on Sources.

What still needs you

  • Weight is published on 21 of 59 listings. 38 rows still publish no gram weight in static HTML and remain blank, never estimated.
  • Surface XRF alone is not enough (GIA 2024–2025). Pair XRF with calibrated gram weight and hydrostatic specific gravity inside the return window to rule out resin-filled electroforming or thick gold-on-gold plating.
  • “Setting only” prices are not complete ring prices. 14 listed products exclude the centre stone.
