Headline finding: karat is everywhere, the word “solid” is rare

Every one of the 43 listings states a karat. 4 of them state “solid gold” in the product text — the rest name a karat and nothing more. That does not make them plated (none of the 43 carries plating language), but the explicit confirmation your checklist asks for is still missing at checkout. What the checklist got right

  • Karat is stated on every listing. All 43 rows name 14K or 18K in the official feed.
  • All four return policies are confirmed in detail. Ross-Simons allows 30 days from receipt and Aurate 14 days from fulfilment; With Clarity refuses returns after a third-party resize and Anna Sheffield charges a 10% restocking fee.
  • Plated metal was excluded, not ranked. Set & Stones' engagement results were gold-filled and were rejected.

What still needs you

  • Weight is the missing number. 36 of 43 listings do not publish grams, so the price-versus-gold-value check cannot be finished for them.
  • Hallmarks cannot be seen online. Ask for a photo of the inside of the band, then have a jeweller test it on arrival.
  • “Setting only” prices are not ring prices. 14 listed products exclude the centre stone.

What the re-read found

  • Weight is not as rare as it looked. Ross-Simons publishes a Gold Weight line; 6 rows gained a real weight across the two P12 sessions, taking coverage from 1 to 7 of 43.
  • Two prices had moved. rs-571223 and rs-943668 no longer list the old values; both were corrected and the drift is logged as F31.
  • Two “impossible” low prices are explainable. $149 and $179 are 0.5 g rings; the flag now says so instead of “cannot be checked” (F06).
