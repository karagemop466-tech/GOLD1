Weight and gold content

The value check needs two numbers: the price, which every seller publishes, and the metal weight in grams, which almost none of them do. This page shows exactly which numbers exist, what they compute to, and where the gap is.

No hallucinations — the rule this whole project runs on

Every price, karat and weight below is copied from the seller's own official product page or feed and linked on the same row. Nothing is estimated, averaged, inferred or remembered. If a value was not confirmed on an official source it is shown as — unverified and listed on the Flagged for review page. Every build is mechanically verified by automated anti-hallucination and security passes (S01–S19 + A01–A10); re-check the live source link before paying.

The formula, and why it is not an appraisal

Fine gold (g) = published grams × (karat ÷ 24)
Gold value (USD) = fine gold (g) × ($4,024.70 ÷ 31.1034768 USD per gram)

Worked from a row on this site: Women's 2mm 14kt Yellow Gold Band at Ross-Simons is listed at $400.00 and publishes 1.7 g at 14K. 1.7 g × (14 ÷ 24) = 0.992 g of fine gold; × $129.40/g = $128.32. The asking price is 3.1× that metal value.

Labour, setting, stones, hallmarking, retail margin and sales tax are all outside this number, which is why finished jewellery normally sells for 2–6× its melt value. A multiple under 1 would mean the price is below the metal — so treat it as a data error, not a bargain. Spot price: $4,024.70/ozt at 2026-10-23T09:59:43Z (gold-api.com ↗). What to do with this

  1. Ask the seller for the weight in grams for your exact size, in writing, before paying.
  2. Paste their number into the calculator with today's gold price to get the metal floor.
  3. On arrival, have an independent jeweller weigh the ring and XRF-test the karat before you wear or resize it.
  4. If the measured weight is materially below what the seller stated, that is your return reason — keep the written statement and the receipt.
