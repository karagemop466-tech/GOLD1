Legal context — each claim with its official source

ClaimSourceEvidence quote (fetched 2026-10-22)
A karat-quality marking must be correct: it is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent the quantity or karat fineness of gold in a product.official source ↗§ 23.3(a): 'It is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent the presence of gold or gold alloy in an industry product, or the quantity or karat fineness of gold or gold alloy contained in the product...'
The word 'Gold' must be immediately preceded by a correct karat designation of at least equal conspicuousness (e.g. '14 Kt. Gold'), or be described by the karat designation alone (e.g. '14Kt.').official source ↗§ 23.3(c)(1): '...immediately preceded by a correct designation of the karat fineness of the alloy, and such karat designation is of equal conspicuousness as the word "Gold" (for example, "14 Karat Gold", "14 K. Gold", "14 Kt. Gold", "9 Karat Gold", or "9 Kt. Gold").'
Hollow gold products must NOT be described as 'solid' gold.official source ↗§ 23.3(c)(1): '...Such products should not be marked or described as "solid" or as being solidly of gold or of a gold alloy.'
A quality mark (e.g. '14K') must be accompanied by the trademark or name of the manufacturer/dealer (US National Stamping Act).official source ↗§ 23.10: 'The National Stamping Act provides that any person... who applies or causes to be applied a quality mark... shall apply to the article the trademark or name of such person. [15 U.S.C. 297]'
US fineness tolerance: assayed as one piece including solder, the actual fineness may not be below the mark by more than 3/1000 (watchcases, flatware) or 7/1000 (other articles — rings included).official source ↗§295: '...shall not be less by more than three one-thousandth parts, in the case of a watchcase or flatware, or than seven one-thousandth parts, in the case of any other such article, than the fineness indicated by the mark...'
Falsely marked gold ware cannot lawfully be imported/exported or shipped in US interstate commerce.official source ↗§294: 'It shall be unlawful... to import or export or cause to be imported into or exported from the United States... any article of merchandise... made in whole or in part of gold or silver... and... indicating... that the gold or silver or alloy... is of a greater degree of fineness than the actual fineness...'
Gold-plated / gold-filled / rolled gold plate / gold overlay / electroplate are SURFACE coatings, not solid gold; each term has specific FTC fineness and durability conditions and requires the karat designation of the plating.official source ↗§ 23.3(b)(5)-(8) and (c)(2): mechanical plating terms require 'a plating of gold alloy of not less than 10 karat fineness and of reasonable durability... when such plating constitutes at least 1/40th of the weight of the metal in the entire article...'
UK hallmarking is performed by independent assay offices (e.g. the Goldsmiths' Company Assay Office, hallmarking since 1300); standard gold finenesses include 9, 14, 18, 22 ct, 990 and 999.official source ↗'...hallmarking began... since 1300 when we first began testing and hallmarking precious metals.' Calculator metal list: 'Gold (14 Carat), Gold (18 carat), Gold (22 carat), Gold (9 carat), Gold (990), Gold (999)'.
Diamond/metal weight claims are regulated: it is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent weight; a carat = 200 mg exactly.official source ↗§ 23.18(a),(b): 'It is unfair or deceptive to misrepresent the weight of a diamond.' 'A carat is a standard unit of weight for a diamond and is equivalent to 200 milligrams (1/5 gram).'

⚠ Watch out

  • SECONDARY-SOURCE DISCREPANCY (found during hallucination audit): several retail blogs claim US law allows a '0.5 karat' tolerance below the stamp (e.g. 'a 14K stamp can assay 13.5K'). The official statute (15 U.S.C. §295, as amended 1976) instead sets whole-article tolerances of 7/1000 fineness (~0.17 karat at 14K) for rings. Do not rely on the 0.5-karat figure — see Flagged item F9.
  • A stamp alone is not proof. The FTC Guides regulate CLAIMS; independent verification of an individual piece requires assay (e.g. XRF) by an independent jeweler/assay office.
