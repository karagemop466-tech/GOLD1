F9highPopular '0.5 karat tolerance' claim contradicted by the official statute
Multiple retail blogs claim a US 'half-karat' tolerance below a gold stamp (e.g. 'a 14K ring may assay 13.5K'). The official National Gold and Silver Stamping Act as amended (15 U.S.C. §295, govinfo) instead sets whole-article tolerances of 3/1000 fineness (watchcases, flatware) or 7/1000 fineness (all other articles) below the mark — about 0.17 karat at 14K, not 0.5. The 1976 amendment notes confirm the older 'one-half of one carat' language was REPLACED. Rely on the statute, not the blogs.
“§295: '...shall not be less by more than three one-thousandth parts, in the case of a watchcase or flatware, or than seven one-thousandth parts, in the case of any other such article, than the fineness indicated by the mark...' (Amendments 1976: substituted 'three one-thousandth parts' for 'one-half of one carat'...)”
https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/294 ↗

F11mediumFTC removed the 10K minimum in 2018 — older checklists are out of date
The project checklist (and much retail guidance) assumes 10K is the US legal minimum to call something 'gold'. The FTC's 2018 revision of the Jewelry Guides removed that threshold; the Guides' own example list now includes '9 Karat Gold' as an acceptable designation when the karat fineness is correctly and conspicuously disclosed. Consequence for shoppers: check the DISCLOSED karat and the actual marking, not just the word 'gold'. (Secondary confirmations: JCK and National Jeweler coverage of the 2018 revision.)
“16 CFR 23.3(c)(1): '...for example, "14 Karat Gold", "14 K. Gold", "14 Kt. Gold", "9 Karat Gold", or "9 Kt. Gold".'”
https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B… ↗ https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements… ↗
