Verification

How each number earned its place.

Verification here is not a promise that the data is complete. It is a promise that nothing was invented, that every value is traceable, and that everything unchecked is labelled.

The six passes, run before every publish

python3 tools/verify_data.py — 1,761 assertions on the current dataset, 0 errors and 0 warnings when last run on 2026-10-22.

  1. Schema pass. Every row carries id, retailer, product, URL, source, price, price basis, purity, weight note, hallmark note, checked date and flags. Placeholders (“null”, “TBD”, empty strings) fail the build.
  2. Source pass. Every row's source must be https, must resolve to the retailer's own domain, and must exist in the source registry. A price with no registered source cannot be published.
  3. Price pass. Price must be a positive whole dollar amount; the display string must contain that exact number; “From” pricing must say so; the checked date must be real and no more than seven days old.
  4. Evidence pass. A weight may only be present if it has its own source URL. A missing weight must say “no gram weight published” and carry flag F-04. A setting-only price must carry F-06. No exceptions.
  5. Flag pass. Every referenced flag exists, every flag has a severity, a status and an official source, and retailer-level flags point at vetted or explicitly-unvetted sellers.
  6. Statistics pass. The headline numbers are recomputed from the rows and compared. If a summary says 9 setting-only rows and the data says 8, the build fails — a wrong statistic is a hallucination even when every row is correct.

Hallucination checks that actually caught something

0 errors remain in the current audit; 4 were caught and fixed during development. The parser found a wildcard row reference, two flag sources pointing at hosts outside the approved list, and a purity count that said 9 × 14K / 4 × 18K when the rows said 10 × 14K / 3 × 18K. All four were fixed before this page existed.

2 snippet prices were wrong. A search result showed $925 for a Blue Nile setting whose live page said $1,420 (F-02), and $2,800 for a Mejuri ring whose live page said $2,900 (F-03). This is why snippets are never used as evidence.

1 page contradicts itself. A Blue Nile 18K ring's description says 14k white gold (F-01). It is published, not hidden.

Primary sources for the law

  • 16 CFR Part 23 — Guides for the Jewelry, Precious Metals, and Pewter Industries (eCFR, current). Establishes that a gold description must be at least 10 karat and governs quality marks.
  • 83 FR 40659 (2018-08-16). Source of the quoted Note 2 to §23.10: the National Stamping Act requires the maker's trademark or name beside a quality mark.

Read with care: these rules govern how sellers may mark and describe jewellery. They do not certify that any particular ring is the purity it claims, which is why independent testing after delivery is still the last step.

What was not verified

  • Seller reputation. No BBB profile, complaint record or independent review set was read (F-10).
  • Return windows at Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth (F-08, F-09).
  • Credit-card dispute rights. Recommended, but no official citation captured (F-12).
  • Regulatory text was confirmed through official excerpts and index entries, not full section reads (F-11).
  • Physical properties. No ring was weighed, XRF-tested or inspected. Every weight and hallmark cell in the dataset is empty because sellers do not publish them.
