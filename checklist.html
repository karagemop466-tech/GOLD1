The checklist, scored

Your original checklist, line by line. Each line says what was checked, what was found, and what is left for you to do at the counter.

No hallucinations — the rule this whole project runs on

Every price, karat and weight below is copied from the seller's own product feed and linked on the same row. Nothing is estimated, averaged or remembered. If a value was not confirmed on an official source it is shown as — unverified and listed on the Flagged for review page. Re-check against the source link before you buy: prices change without notice.

Gold purity: confirm the karat — 10K, 14K, 18K. Higher karat = more gold.

verified

All 30 rows state a karat in the seller's own text and the karat is on the row with the source link. The set is 14K and 18K only; no 10K pieces survived the shortlist.

How it was checked

What to do

2

Solid gold: the listing must explicitly say solid gold, not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, overlay or gold-tone.

open issue

0 of 30 listings contain the word "solid". None contains plating language either. So the shortlist is clear of plated products, but the explicit confirmation the checklist demands is missing everywhere. One Aurate product does sell a vermeil variant alongside solid gold, which is the exact trap this rule exists to catch.

How it was checked
Searched the captured product text of all 30 rows for solid / plated / vermeil / filled / overlay / gold-tone, and inspected every metal option in the Aurate variants.
What to do
Get one written sentence from the seller: "solid 14K gold, not plated, not vermeil, not gold-filled." Keep it with the receipt.

3

Hallmark: a 10K/14K/18K stamp plus a maker's/manufacturer's mark.

not verifiable online

No retailer page retrieved states the hallmark or maker's mark that will be struck inside the band. This cannot be confirmed from a web page at all, so nothing is claimed.

How it was checked
Looked for stamp/hallmark wording in each product description; found none. US marking rules live in 16 CFR Part 23, including § 23.3 (misrepresentation as to gold content) and § 23.10 (quality marks).
What to do
Ask for a close-up photo of the inside of the band before paying, then have an independent jeweller read the stamp on arrival.

4

Weight: check the ring's weight in grams — a legitimate seller should provide it.

open issue

1 of 30 listings publishes a metal weight (Aurate Modern Cigar Band, 2.6–4 g). The other 29 show a blank, not an estimate. This is the single biggest gap in the whole project.

How it was checked
Read every captured product description for gram weights; compared against the feed's variant data.
What to do
Email the seller for the gram weight of your size. If they cannot or will not give it, that is your answer.

6

Price: compare the price with the ring's gold weight and current gold value. An unusually cheap price is a red flag.

partly verified

The gold price is recorded with a publisher timestamp ($4,028.10/ozt on 2026-10-22) and the calculator does grams × karat ÷ 24 × price. For 29 of 30 rows the comparison cannot be completed because the weight is unpublished — so the site shows no multiple rather than a made-up one. Two Ross-Simons pieces at $149 and $179 are flagged as value-unverifiable for exactly this reason.

How it was checked
Automated melt-value test: for any row with a published weight, the asking price must not be below the gold value. The arithmetic is published on the calculator page.
What to do
Get grams, then run the calculator. Below 1× melt is a red flag; 2–6× is a normal retail multiple.

7

Return policy: make sure you can return it after inspecting it, including exclusions on resized or custom rings.

verified

All four return policies were read in full. Ross-Simons: 30 days from receipt, and the exact exclusion your checklist warns about — merchandise 'altered, or resized by a jeweler other than Ross-Simons' cannot be returned. Aurate: 14 days from fulfilment for US orders, 30 days for exchanges, engraved and international sales final. With Clarity: 30 days, but resizing or repair by another jeweller voids it and a missing diamond certificate costs $250. Anna Sheffield: two weeks from delivery, with a 10% restocking fee (max $500) and engraved or bespoke orders final sale.

How it was checked
Read each retailer's official refund-policy page and copied the exact conditions into data/retailers.json, including the resizing and custom-order exclusions.
What to do
Do not resize before you decide to keep it, and never let a third-party jeweller touch it while the return window is open. Keep certificates and original packaging.

8

Payment: prefer a credit card for purchase protection; avoid irreversible payment methods.

your action

How it was checked
No value was inferred. This item is advice, not data.

Pay by credit card. Never wire transfer, and avoid anything that removes your chargeback rights.

11

Best verification: ask for XRF testing or another professional gold-purity test; do not rely solely on the stamp.

your action

XRF is a physical test performed on the ring. No retailer page retrieved offers or publishes an XRF result per piece, so no purity claim beyond the seller's karat statement is made anywhere on this site.

How it was checked
Searched the captured product text for XRF / assay; nothing was found, so nothing was claimed.
What to do
Ask the jeweller for XRF on the band (not just the setting) and keep the printout with the receipt.
